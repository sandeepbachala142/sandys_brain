# (C) Yush — Volume Profile Deep Dive

*Official Yush Capital framework for reading market structure using VP on NQ and ES.*

---

## Why Volume Profile

> *"Most traders watch price. Price tells you where the market has been, but it doesn't tell you where business actually got done. Volume Profile flips the chart on its side and answers a different question: at every price, how much was traded?"*

Areas of heavy volume → **magnets and shelves** (price sticks and returns)
Areas of thin volume → **vacuums** (price travels fast)

---

## Why the Prior Day's RTH Session (Not Overnight)

The 24-hour futures session is misleading. For NQ and ES:

> *"The overwhelming majority of the day's contracts trade during the New York Regular Trading Hours session — 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM ET. That's where the institutions, funds, and real two-sided business actually print."*

**Two practical consequences:**

1. **The prior day's RTH profile is the map.** It shows where previous day's real participants agreed price was fair (Value Area), where they were most active (POC), and where they refused to trade (HVNs and LVNs outside value area).

2. **Overnight ranges = exploration, not commitment.** When price drifts away from prior RTH value area in Globex on light volume → treat as a probe, not a directional move. The real test comes when NY opens.

**The pre-market question:** *"Where is price right now relative to yesterday's auction?"*

---

## When the Overnight Profile DOES Matter

Normally ignore Globex. But two specific situations make it a real reference:

### 1. Market is at All-Time Highs
No prior structure overhead — no old shelves, no prior value areas at this price. The overnight profile is the ONLY auction history above the prior RTH high.
- Globex POC, VAH, VAL become working reference levels until NY opens
- At ATH: **overnight structure IS the structure**

### 2. Large Overnight Move with Wide Range (Catalyst)
News, geopolitics, an overnight event — real participants traded that move, a meaningful profile got built.
- Treat overnight POC and value area as live reference levels for the open
- First reaction at the overnight POC, VAH, or VAL when NY opens tells you whether the move will be accepted or faded

**Outside these two cases → prior day's RTH profile is the only reference that matters.**

---

## The 5 Levels to Mark Every Morning

From the **prior RTH session only**:

1. **POC (Point of Control)** — single price with most volume traded yesterday. The auction's center of gravity.
2. **VAH (Value Area High)** — upper boundary of the 70% volume range.
3. **VAL (Value Area Low)** — lower boundary of the 70% volume range.
4. **HVNs (High Volume Nodes)** — thick horizontal shelves inside or near the profile. Price tends to *stick* here.
5. **LVNs (Low Volume Nodes)** — thin spots in the profile. Price tends to *travel through* these quickly — acceptance gaps.

> *"That's it. Five reference points. Clean chart, no clutter."*

---

## Reading the Developing Session — Open Scenarios

**The key tell each morning:** the first reaction at yesterday's VAH or VAL.

That single test telegraphs whether the day will be:
- A one-timeframe trend day
- A range day inside yesterday's value
- A reversal back into balance

### Scenario 1: Open Inside Yesterday's Value Area
→ Expect rotational/balance behavior
→ Trade is initiative only when price breaks value area extremes with conviction
→ **Default: fade the edges**

### Scenario 2: Open Above Yesterday's VAH (Gap Up)
Two possibilities:
- **Buyers defend VAH on first pullback** → yesterday's value left behind, today's auction exploring higher → **Long bias**
- **Sellers immediately drive back into value area** → gap fills → rotation toward POC, possibly down to VAL → **Short bias**

### Scenario 3: Open Below Yesterday's VAL (Gap Down)
Mirror image of Scenario 2.
- Sellers defend VAL on first pullback → **Short bias**
- Buyers reclaim back into value area → rotation toward POC → **Long bias**

## Inflection Points — Where the Next Decision Gets Made

> *"A volume profile inflection point is a price level where a trend has a meaningfully elevated probability of pausing, reversing, or accelerating."*

### Category 1 — Low Volume Nodes (LVNs)

LVNs are the thin spots — price moved through them quickly the first time because there was no two-sided interest. That's exactly why they matter on a retest.

**When price returns to an LVN — two outcomes, both tradeable:**

**Rejection (higher probability):**
- Price approaches the LVN and stalls
- Nobody wanted to trade there before, nobody does now
- Strong-handed traders use the LVN as a wall and defend it
- Enter at the edge, stop on the far side

**Slice-through:**
- LVN fails → price travels fast to the next HVN (no volume to slow it)
- Trade the vacuum to the next shelf
- *"The thinner the node, the cleaner the level."*

### Category 2 — Multi-Day / Multi-Week HVNs

A shelf that keeps getting reinforced day after day is **structural**. Real institutional business has been done here repeatedly.

**What multi-day HVNs mean:**
- **Sustained agreement** — big participants have been comfortable transacting here for extended periods. Fair value at higher timeframe.
- **Strong magnet AND strong defender** — price gets pulled back repeatedly, tends to hold on first test. Above current price: caps rallies. Below: catches selloffs.
- **A break is significant** — slicing through a multi-week HVN is a real structural event. Usually signals the market is leaving that value zone and seeking a new one. A close above/below often sets up a multi-day directional move.

> *"The longer the HVN has been building and the more sessions that have contributed to it, the more weight I give it."*

## Profile Shapes That Telegraph Reversals

### P-Shaped Profile → Bearish Reversal Signal
- Thin, fast move **higher** followed by consolidation/distribution at the top
- **Thin tail** underneath = very little volume traded on the way up (LVN leg)
- **Fat distribution at the top** = longs getting trapped, sellers stepping in, can't push higher
- Most common after: short squeeze or stop run higher
- Resolution: usually back down into prior balance
- **Only valid if session CLOSES with price in the distribution (the fat part)**

### b-Shaped Profile → Bullish Reversal Signal
- Thin, fast move **lower** followed by consolidation/distribution at the lows
- **Thin tail** above = very little volume on the way down (LVN leg)
- **Fat distribution at the bottom** = sellers exhausted, buyers stepping in and reversing
- Most common after: flush, capitulation, or stop run lower
- **Only valid if session CLOSES with price in the distribution**

**Two important notes:**
- If price has already broken out of the distribution before the close → setup is gone
- Shape is a **probability tilt, not a guarantee** — order flow and delta confirmation still matter

## Execution: Framework + Trigger

> *"This is the part most people miss. Volume profile is not an entry signal. It never has been."*

### THE FRAMEWORK — Volume Profile
Volume profile's job: tell you **WHERE**.
- Maps the auction
- Identifies where business has been done and where it hasn't
- POC, VAH, VAL, HVNs, LVNs = the only levels on the chart
- Gives you the playing field
- **Does NOT tell you when to click the button**

### THE TRIGGER — Order Flow
Order flow's job: tell you **WHEN**.
- Once VP drops you into the right neighborhood → question shifts to the tape
- Is there absorption? Is delta diverging? Are aggressive sellers getting filled into passive bids?
- **No order flow confirmation at a profile level = no trade — even if the level is "perfect"**

> **"Volume profile says where. Order flow says when. Neither one works without the other."**

**Combined:** Profile filters the noise down to a handful of locations that matter. Order flow tells you which of those locations are actually being defended in real time.

## Pre-Market Routine (Yush's Exact Process)

1. Mark yesterday's RTH POC, VAH, VAL, plus visible HVNs and LVNs
2. If price is at ATH, OR overnight ran a wide range on catalyst → add Globex POC and value area
3. Identify any multi-day or multi-week HVNs sitting near current price on the composite profile
4. Note where overnight has traded relative to those levels
5. Decide which scenario is in play at the open:
   - Open inside value → rotation
   - Open above VAH → acceptance higher or rejection back
   - Open below VAL → mirror

---

## Intraday Execution Rules

- **VP sets the where.** No setup taken outside an inflection level — LVNs, multi-day HVNs, value area edges. No random pullbacks. No mid-range guesses.
- **Order flow triggers the when.** Absorption, delta divergence, aggressive flips at the level = green lights. Without them, the level is just a line.
- **Stops** go beyond the level, sized to the structure of the zone.
- **Targets** are the next inflection level out — opposing value area edge, next HVN shelf, or other side of an LVN vacuum.

---

## What Yush Avoids

❌ Trading in the **middle of a value area** with no levels nearby — that's chop by definition
❌ Treating **overnight highs and lows** with same weight as RTH levels (unless ATH or wide-range catalyst)
❌ Pulling the trigger on a profile level **without order flow confirmation** — the level is the invitation, not the trade
❌ Trading **against a clear P or b shape** just because price looks "extended" — the shape IS the extension

## Bottom Line (Verbatim from Yush)

> *"Volume profile isn't an indicator. It's a record of where the market did business and where it didn't. For NQ and ES, the prior day's RTH session is the primary reference because that's where the real volume traded — with two exceptions: at ATH, and after wide-range overnight moves, the Globex profile becomes the working map. Mark yesterday's POC, VAH, VAL, HVNs, and LVNs. Watch the open's first reaction against them. Identify the inflection points — LVN rejections and multi-day HVN shelves — where the next decision will be made. Use shape (P, b, balance) to read what just happened in the auction. Then let order flow trigger the entry."*

> **"Profile is the framework. Order flow is the trigger. That's the entire game."**
