# (C) Yush — Volume Profile Foundations

---

## 1. Day Types — Know What Kind of Day You're In

Before you trade, read the developing profile to understand the day type. This determines your bias and what setups are available.

---

### NON-TREND Days (Balance)
Price is in balance — two-sided auction. Fade the extremes. Avoid breakout trades.

| Day Type | Profile Shape | What It Means | How to Trade |
|----------|--------------|---------------|--------------|
| **Normal Day** | Wide D-shape, fat bell curve | Balanced, fair value found. Buyers and sellers agree. | Fade extremes back to POC. No breakout trades. |
| **Neutral Day** | D-shape with thin initial balance line | Very balanced. Opens with a small range, expands both ways equally. | Extreme fades only. Expect chop. Low trade day. |
| **Normal Variation** | Slightly skewed D-shape | Balance but with a slight directional lean. One side pushed more. | Can lean toward the skew side but expect rotation back. |

---

### TREND Days (Imbalance)
Price is out of balance — one-sided auction. Trade with the trend, not against it. These days produce the big moves.

| Day Type | Profile Shape | What It Means | How to Trade |
|----------|--------------|---------------|--------------|
| **Standard Trend** | Thin, elongated profile — single distribution | Strong directional move. Institutions driving price one way. | Trade pullbacks in the direction of trend. No fades. |
| **Double Distribution** | Two separate bell curves stacked (B-shape) | Price auctioned in one zone, broke out, then auctioned in a second zone. | Trade the breakout between the two distributions. LVN between them = fast move. |

---

### Reading the Day Type in Real Time

```
First 30-60 mins (Initial Balance):
→ Wide IB + balanced profile → likely NON-TREND → fade extremes
→ Narrow IB + one-sided profile → likely TREND day developing → follow direction
→ Price extending one way with thin profile → Standard Trend → ride it
→ Two separate clusters forming → Double Distribution → trade the bridge LVN
```

## 2. Low Volume Zones (LVN) vs High Volume Zones (HVN)

This is the foundation of WHY the LVN Model works.

### HVN — High Volume Node (Heavy Volume Zone)
- Lots of historical trading at this price
- Acts as **magnet** — price returns here, slows down, rotates
- Support/resistance that holds
- Where Yush's LVN Model **clusters** form

### LVN — Low Volume Node (Low Volume Zone)
- Very little historical trading at this price
- Price moves **fast** through these zones
- Why: no resting orders = no friction = fast delivery
- The thin area **between two HVN clusters** is your LVN

### The Visual Rule
```
[HVN Cluster Above]
       ↑ ↓  fast move up/down
[LVN — thin, barely any volume]
       ↑ ↓  fast move up/down  
[HVN Cluster Below]
```

**When price breaks out of an HVN into the LVN → it will move FAST to the next HVN.**
This is why Yush's LVN Model works — you're entering just after the breakout, targeting the speed of the LVN.

## 3. Profile Shapes — Read the Bias at a Glance

Before looking at any indicator, the shape of the profile alone tells you who's in control.

### D-Shaped Profile
```
     |
     |  ████
     | ███████
     |██████████
     | ███████
     |  ████
     |
```
- **Balanced** — buyers and sellers at equilibrium
- Price found fair value, both sides traded equally
- **Bias: Neutral** — fade extremes back to POC
- Expect rotation, not trend

---

### B-Shaped Profile
```
     |
     |  ████
     | ███████  ← upper distribution
     |  ████
     |    ──────
     |  ████
     | ████████  ← lower distribution
     |  ████
     |
```
- **Double Distribution** — price auctioned in two separate zones
- Gap/LVN between the two clusters
- **Bias: Directional** — the gap between clusters tells the story. Upper cluster = recent acceptance. Lower = prior acceptance.
- Watch for breakout between the two — **LVN between them = fast bridge**

---

### P-Shaped Profile
```
     |
     |  ████████
     | ██████████  ← fat top (buyers accepted high prices)
     |  ████████
     |  ████
     | ████
     |  ██
     |   █  ← thin tail (sellers briefly pushed low, rejected)
     |
```
- **Buying tail** — sellers pushed down but couldn't hold it. Buyers swept back up and built value at the TOP.
- Thin tail at bottom = SSL swept, no follow through
- Fat top = buyers absorbed everything, value formed high
- **Bias: Bullish** — price wants to go higher. Don't short into this. Look for LBF setups at VAL.

## 4. How These Connect to Yush's Setups

| Concept | Which Setup It Feeds |
|---------|---------------------|
| HVN = cluster edge | LVN Model — the cluster you're defending |
| LVN = thin zone | LVN Model — the space between clusters = your target |
| D-shaped day | LAF/LBF — balanced day, VAH/VAL sweeps likely |
| P-shaped profile | LBF long — buyers dominate, VAL dips are traps |
| Double Distribution | LVN Model — trade the LVN gap between two clusters |
| Standard Trend day | Don't fade. Ride with the LVN Model in trend direction |

---

## 5. Pre-Session Profile Read — Quick Routine

Before every session:

```
1. Look at the previous session profile shape
   D-shape  → balance day, fades available
   P-shape  → bullish bias, buy dips at VAL (LBF)
   B-shape  → double distribution, LVN in the middle = target

2. Identify clusters (HVNs) above and below current price

3. Identify the LVN gaps between clusters — these are your speed zones

4. Mark VAH, VAL, POC on the developing session

5. Ask: Is today shaping up as balance (D) or trend (elongated)?
   → If balance → wait for VAH/VAL sweeps (LAF/LBF setups)
   → If trend   → wait for LVN breakout + defend (LVN Model)
```
