## Trade 1 — ESM6 | SHORT | ❌ STOPPED OUT

**Instrument:** ESM6 (3-min)
**Direction:** Short
**Entry:** ~7391–7392
**Stop:** Hit (above the green bubble zone)
**Result:** Loss

### What Sandy Saw
- Big green bubbles (aggressive buyers) around 7390–7398
- Heavy positive delta at the level
- Price broke above the buyers, then came back and closed below the delta cluster
- Identified it as: buyers trapped → LAF short → entered short

### What Actually Happened
This was a **LBF setup misread as LAF**.

The green bubbles were appearing at a **LOW after a major sell-off** — not at a high/resistance. That completely changes the read:

| Signal Location | Correct Read | Trade |
|----------------|-------------|-------|
| Green bubbles at HIGH/resistance | Bearish absorption — buyers trapped | SHORT (LAF) |
| Green bubbles at LOW after flush | **Bullish absorption — sellers exhausted** | **LONG (LBF)** ✅ |

Sandy correctly identified the signal (bubbles + delta). The mistake was misreading the **context of where the signal appeared.**

### Supporting Evidence Sandy Missed
- M: 7390.35 midpoint was right there providing support
- VP showed fat HVN cluster on the left around that zone — institutional shelf
- Price had already made a massive sell-off = exhaustion setup, not continuation

### What the Correct Trade Was
**LONG off that absorption** — not short. Green bubbles at lows after a flush = LBF = buy the reclaim.

### Mistake Category
> ❌ **Mistake #1 — Wrong directional read on absorption signal**
> Identified the signal correctly but misread the location = entered opposite direction

## Trade 2 — NQM6 | LONG | ✅ Small Win (Exit too cautious initially, actually correct)

**Instrument:** NQM6 (2-min)
**Direction:** Long
**Entry:** ~28820–28830
**Exit:** ~28880–28900
**Result:** Small win (~60–80 pts NQ)

### What Sandy Saw
- Heavy sellers (red bubbles) all the way down
- 143-contract red bubble at the absolute session low (~28811)
- Price did NOT continue lower despite the massive sell print
- Recognized as LBF (Look Below and Fail) — heavy selling absorbed, no follow-through
- Entered long on the break back up
- Exited when momentum stalled

### What Sandy Did Right ✅
- Correctly identified absorption signal (red bubble at low + no follow-through)
- Correct direction for the setup (LONG on LBF)
- Exited before price reversed back down — good instinct
- Did NOT hold hoping for more

### What Happened After Exit
Price continued DOWN to L: 28742 — another 150 points lower. The exit was actually correct.

### The Bigger Issue — Day Type
This was a **strong trend day** — thin elongated profile, OL: 29113 broken, M: 29019 broken, consistent lower highs and lower lows.

On a trend day:
- Counter-trend LBF bounces are **small and temporary**
- The 143 bubble was a pause, not a true reversal
- The correct trades were **LAF shorts on every bounce back to resistance**

Sandy caught the right signal but traded it on the wrong day type. On a balance day, this LBF would have run 150–200 pts. On a trend day, it gave 60–80 and reversed.

### Mistake Category
> ⚠️ **Mistake #2 — Trading counter-trend LBF on a trend day**
> Right signal, right direction, wrong day type context. Resulted in a small win instead of a big loss, but the lesson is: identify the day type first.

## Mentor Note

> You're learning fast Sandy. Two trades, two different mistakes, two different lessons. That's actually good — you're not making the same mistake twice in one session. But here's the hard truth:
>
> You're still learning WHAT the signal means without fully understanding WHERE it matters. The signal (bubbles + delta) is the easy part. The context (day type, location in structure, trend vs balance) is the hard part. That's what separates profitable traders from ones who are "almost there."
>
> Next session — before you look at bubbles, ask yourself two questions first:
> 1. **What day type is this?** (trend or balance)
> 2. **Where are we in the structure?** (at a high, at a low, mid-range)
>
> Then look at the bubbles. The order matters.
